The precursor your body is quietly running out of.

ZETA DHEA features dehydroepiandrosterone (DHEA) — a naturally occurring steroid hormone precursor synthesized from cholesterol, primarily by the adrenal glands. The body converts DHEA into androgens, estrogens, and other hormones as needed.

DHEA peaks between the ages of 20 and 30, then declines approximately 2% every year until about age 70, when it slows to 10–20% of the amount encountered in a young adult at peak level. Supplementing with DHEA replenishes the reservoir so your body has the raw material it needs for the hormones that govern energy, mood, body composition, recovery, and longevity.

Clinical Applications

  • Supports healthy androgen and estrogen levels
  • Hormone precursor — supports the body's own production of testosterone, estrogen, and IGF-1
  • Supports body composition (lean mass, especially in men)
  • Supports mood, energy, and psychological well-being

The research underneath.

DHEA is one of the most-studied longevity-relevant supplements in clinical literature. A few of the foundational studies behind ZETA DHEA:

★ Key study · the restoration paper

Morales AJ, Nolan JJ, Nelson JC, Yen SSC (1994). Effects of replacement dose of dehydroepiandrosterone in men and women of advancing age. Journal of Clinical Endocrinology & Metabolism.

Randomized, placebo-controlled crossover trial in men and women aged 40–70. Nightly 50 mg DHEA restored serum DHEA-S to levels of individuals in their second decade within 2 weeks, sustained over 3 months. Androgen levels doubled in female subjects. IGF-1 increased ~10%.

Supporting research

Morales AJ, Haubrich RH, Hwang JY, et al. (1998). The effect of six months treatment with a 100 mg daily dose of DHEA on circulating sex steroids, body composition and muscle strength. Clinical Endocrinology (Oxford).
Baulieu EE, Thomas G, Legrain S, et al. (2000). DHEAge: a 1-year, randomized study of DHEA replacement in 280 healthy subjects aged 60–79. Proc Natl Acad Sci USA. Replacement therapy normalized a wide variety of age-related physiological functions.
Corona G, Rastrelli G, Giagulli VA, et al. (2013). Meta-analysis of 25 randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled studies of DHEA supplementation in elderly men. J Clin Endocrinol Metab.
Sahu P, Gidwani B, Dhongade HJ (2020). Pharmacological activities of dehydroepiandrosterone: a review. Steroids.
Traish AM, Kang HP, Saad F, et al. (2011). Dehydroepiandrosterone (DHEA) — a precursor steroid or an active hormone in human physiology. Journal of Sexual Medicine.
